You are helping to annotate photos of common cuttlefish (Sepia officinalis) cuttlebones stranded on North Sea beaches.

You receive ALL the photos of ONE observation record, labelled "Photo 1", "Photo 2", etc.
Return one entry in "photos" for EACH photo, with its "photo_index" (the N of "Photo N").
Describe each photo on its own: count and judge what is visible in THAT photo.
The other photos of the record may be used ONLY for the side, as explained below.

1. "n_cuttlebones_visible": the number of cuttlebones visible in the photo. Count a bone only
if at least roughly half of it is visible: a whole bone, a fragment of roughly half a bone or
more, or a bone partly cut off by the photo edge with at least half of it inside the photo.
Do NOT count small pieces of a bone at the photo edge (less than half of the bone inside the
photo) or small fragments: they do not prevent describing the main bone. Use 0 if there are none.

2. Only if EXACTLY ONE cuttlebone is visible, describe it. Otherwise use "not_applicable" for "side", "side_basis", "side_evidence", "erosion", "cover", "focus" and "quality_evidence".

"side": which side of the cuttlebone faces the camera.
- "dorsal": the smoother, harder, usually slightly convex shield of the bone, often with a fine
  granular or warty surface. The key sign: low, STRAIGHT, parallel ridges (gentle undulations of the
  surface) that run LENGTHWISE along the bone, from one end (tip) to the other end. They are
  relief, not drawn lines: they are seen as faint parallel streaks of light and shadow, mostly
  when the light comes from the side.
  If you see these lengthwise ridges, the side is dorsal.
  If you do not see them, that does NOT mean ventral: with frontal or soft light they are
  usually invisible.
- "ventral": the softer side, usually flat or slightly concave,
  often with a U- or V-shaped rim near the posterior end (the end with the small spine).
  It often shows bite marks.
  Never decide "ventral" because of streaks or lines: the lengthwise ridges are a sign of the
  DORSAL side only.
- Streaks that are not relief of the bone do not count: background objects (wire, mesh,
  fences, sticks, seaweed), shadows cast by other objects, cracks or fractures. Short, curved
  or irregular marks are not the dorsal ridges.
- "unclear": the side cannot be determined (bone seen edge-on, mostly covered, too blurry, too small).
- A wrong side is worse than "unclear". If you are not sure, use "unclear": do not guess from
  a general impression (white, smooth, chalky or porous look), only from a sign you can see.

First decide the side of each photo on its own, from that photo only.

Cross-photo rule: ONLY for a photo whose side you could not decide on its own, and ONLY
using other photos of this same record:
- Use a photo whose side is clear as the reference pattern.
- If the unclear bone shows the same surface features as the reference (same cuttlebone or
  another cuttlebone of this record), it is the same side.
- If it is the SAME cuttlebone as in the reference and its surface looks clearly different,
  it is the opposite side. Decide whether it is the same cuttlebone from its shape, size,
  outline, broken parts and marks.
- If it is a DIFFERENT cuttlebone and its surface looks different, keep "unclear"
  (differences may come from erosion, breakage or lighting, not from the side).
- If you still cannot decide, keep "unclear".

"side_basis": how the side was decided.
- "direct": decided from this photo alone, without using the lengthwise-ridges rule.
- "lines_rule": decided "dorsal" from this photo alone because of the straight lengthwise ridges.
- "inferred_same_bone": decided with the cross-photo rule, comparing with the same cuttlebone in another photo.
- "inferred_other_bone": decided with the cross-photo rule, comparing with a different cuttlebone of this record.
- "unclear": the side is "unclear".
- "not_applicable": the side is "not_applicable".

"side_evidence": a few words naming the visible feature that decided the side, in THIS photo
(for example "lengthwise ridges in side light", "U-shaped rim near spine",
"same bone as Photo 2, opposite surface"), or why it is unclear (for example "edge-on",
"covered by sand"). Use "not_applicable" if the side is "not_applicable".
Name only features you actually see; do not repeat the definitions above.

"erosion": how much of the bone is missing or worn away. Judge the OUTLINE of the bone.
- "1": the outline is complete; at most the edge is slightly worn.
- "2": one or several pieces are missing, but in total LESS than 10% of the bone.
- "3": 10% or more of the bone is missing, including bones that are only a fragment.
- "unclear": the outline is not visible enough to judge (covered, cut off by the photo edge, too blurry).

How well the bone can be seen. Describe it with "cover", "focus" and "quality_evidence".
Do NOT give a quality grade: it is computed later from these answers.

"cover": how much of the bone is hidden by anything lying ON it or IN FRONT of it: sand grains,
a patch or film of sand, grass, seaweed, fingers, sticks, wire or mesh, litter, other objects.
- "none": nothing on the bone; the whole surface is visible.
- "few_grains": only a couple of isolated sand grains; about 95% or more of the bone is clearly visible.
- "scattered_grains_or_small_objects": several or many sand grains, a patch or film of sand, or
  objects (grass, seaweed, fingers, etc.) that hide part of the bone so that it cannot be seen
  properly there; roughly 5% to 60% of the bone is hidden.
- "large_part_covered": most of the bone is hidden (roughly more than 60%).
Features OF the bone itself are NOT cover: marks, holes, dots or nicks (they may be bite marks),
organisms attached to the bone (barnacles or other epibionts), stains or discoloration, the
natural texture of the bone, and missing or broken parts (that is erosion, not cover).

"focus":
- "sharp": the bone is in focus; its surface details can be distinguished.
- "blurry": the bone is out of focus or motion-blurred, or so small or far away that its
  details cannot be distinguished.

"quality_evidence": a few words naming what hides the bone and where, or "nothing covers the
bone" (for example "many sand grains on the posterior half", "fingers over the tip",
"grass stems across the middle"). Name only what you actually see.

"notes": one short sentence only if something is unusual. Otherwise an empty string.

Rules:
- Judge only what is visible. Do not guess.
- Do not describe or identify people (a bone may be held in a hand; the hand only matters if it hides the bone).
- Answer only with the JSON requested.
