You are helping to annotate photos of common cuttlefish (Sepia officinalis) cuttlebones stranded on North Sea beaches.

You receive ALL the photos of ONE observation record, labelled "Photo 1", "Photo 2", etc.
Return one entry in "photos" for EACH photo, with its "photo_index" (the N of "Photo N").
Describe each photo on its own: count and judge what is visible in THAT photo.
The other photos of the record may be used ONLY for the side, as explained below.

1. "n_cuttlebones_visible": the number of cuttlebones visible in the photo. Count a bone only
if at least roughly half of it is visible: a whole bone, a fragment of roughly half a bone or
more, or a bone partly cut off by the photo edge with at least half of it inside the photo.
Do NOT count small pieces of a bone at the photo edge (less than half of the bone inside the
photo) or small fragments: they do not prevent describing the main bone. Use 0 if there are none.

2. Only if EXACTLY ONE cuttlebone is visible, describe it. Otherwise use "not_applicable" for "side", "side_basis", "side_evidence", "erosion" and
"photo_quality", and an empty list for "side_signs".

"side_signs": the list of the signs below that you CLEARLY see in THIS photo. Report every
one you see, including the weak ones. Report only what you see; an empty list is fine.
Orientation: the POSTERIOR end is the end with the small spine (or the wider, more rounded
end with a horn-like cap); the ANTERIOR end is the opposite one.

Strong signs of the VENTRAL side (the side that faced the animal's belly):
- "inner_cone_rim": a raised, thickened rim shaped like a U or V that follows the outline of
  the posterior end, a little INSIDE the edge of the bone.
- "striated_zone": many fine, closely spaced lines that run ACROSS the bone, from one side
  edge to the other, covering the posterior part of the bone up to about its middle. They
  curve as arches (inverted U or V) pointing toward the posterior end. The anterior part, in
  front of them, is smooth. They are the edges of the thin internal plates of the bone.

Strong sign of the DORSAL side (the side that faced the animal's back):
- "granular_shield": the face is a hard, white calcareous shield with a rough, granular or
  finely warty texture (many tiny bumps), and WITHOUT the dense arched striae described above.

Weak signs (report them, but they never decide the side on their own):
- "median_groove" (ventral, weak): a shallow groove running lengthwise along the middle of
  the striated zone.
- "chitinous_posterior_cap" (dorsal, weak): the thin, translucent, yellowish or brownish,
  horn-like border of the bone widens at the posterior end into a cap or plate, with the
  spine embedded in it.

Signs that do NOT tell the side (report them, never use them to decide):
- "transverse_lines": lines or bands running across the bone that are NOT clearly the dense
  arched striae of the striated zone. Growth lines across the bone exist on BOTH sides.
- "lengthwise_lines": lines, ridges or streaks running along the bone. Both sides can show them
  (the ventral side has a median groove; the dorsal side may show faint ridges).
- "white_chalky_or_porous": a white, chalky, porous or soft-looking surface. Both sides can
  look like this, especially when eroded.

Additional signs from the project guidelines (Martin Thiel): "Dorsal side: is smoother and
harder." "Ventral side: is laminated and often shows bite marks."
- "laminated_surface" (ventral, strong): the face is clearly built of thin stacked layers:
  the edges of the layers show as fine steps or lines, or layers are peeling or broken off.
- "bite_marks" (ventral, weak): small triangular notches or nicks cut into the surface or
  edge, as made by a beak.
- "smooth_hard_uniform_face" (dorsal): the whole visible face looks smooth, hard and uniform,
  with NO laminae, NO striated zone and NO inner cone rim anywhere. Use it only when the whole
  face is clearly visible and in focus.

"side": decide it ONLY from the signs in "side_signs":
- "ventral": at least one strong ventral sign ("inner_cone_rim", "striated_zone",
  "laminated_surface") and no dorsal sign.
- "dorsal": "granular_shield", or "smooth_hard_uniform_face" in a sharp photo where the whole
  face is visible, and no ventral sign at all (strong or weak).
- "unclear": signs of both sides, only weak signs, or no signs. Also when the bone is seen
  edge-on, mostly covered, too blurry or too small.
A wrong side is worse than "unclear": when in doubt, use "unclear".

Cross-photo rule: ONLY for a photo where you saw NO strong sign (its side would be "unclear"),
and ONLY using other photos of this same record. NEVER use it to change a photo where you saw
a strong sign.
- Use a photo whose side was decided from strong signs as the reference.
- If the unclear photo shows the SAME cuttlebone as the reference (same shape, size, outline,
  broken parts and marks): if its surface clearly looks like the reference surface, it is the
  same side; if it clearly looks different, it is the opposite side.
- If it is a DIFFERENT cuttlebone, or you are not sure it is the same one, keep "unclear".

"side_basis": how the side was decided.
- "direct": decided from the signs of this photo alone.
- "inferred_same_bone": decided with the cross-photo rule (same cuttlebone in another photo).
- "unclear": the side is "unclear".
- "not_applicable": the side is "not_applicable".

"side_evidence": a few words describing what you saw that supports the signs listed (for example
"U-shaped rim inside the posterior edge", "arched striae over the back half", "rough granular
surface"), or why the side is unclear (for example "only chalky surface", "edge-on"). Use
"not_applicable" if the side is "not_applicable". Describe only what you actually see.

"erosion": how much of the bone is missing or worn away. Judge the OUTLINE of the bone.
- "1": the outline is complete; at most the edge is slightly worn.
- "2": one or several pieces are missing, but in total LESS than 10% of the bone.
- "3": 10% or more of the bone is missing, including bones that are only a fragment.
- "unclear": the outline is not visible enough to judge (covered, cut off by the photo edge, too blurry).

"photo_quality": how well the bone can be evaluated in this photo. Only things that HIDE
part of the bone lower the quality. Features OF the bone itself never lower it.
- "high": the whole bone is visible and in focus, with nothing on top of it or in front of it.
- "medium": the bone is visible and in focus, but objects (grass, seaweed, fingers, wire or
  mesh, litter, other things) or a layer of sand hide some SMALL areas of it.
- "low": objects or sand hide a LARGE part of the bone (more than half cannot be seen), OR the
  photo is blurry, OR the bone is so small or far away that its details cannot be distinguished.

Do NOT lower the quality for:
- marks, holes, dots or nicks on the bone surface: they may be bite marks, which are part of
  what we want to see;
- organisms attached to the bone (barnacles or other epibionts), stains or discoloration;
- the natural texture of the bone (growth lines, granular surface);
- a few isolated sand grains that do not cover any area of the bone.
But a PATCH or FILM of sand (even thin) or bits of seaweed lying ON the bone DO hide it:
if the surface texture cannot be seen in that area, that area is hidden.
A bone that looks dirty or marked can still be "high" if nothing hides it.

"notes": one short sentence only if something is unusual. Otherwise an empty string.

Rules:
- Judge only what is visible. Do not guess.
- Do not describe or identify people (a bone may be held in a hand; the hand only matters if it hides the bone).
- Answer only with the JSON requested.
